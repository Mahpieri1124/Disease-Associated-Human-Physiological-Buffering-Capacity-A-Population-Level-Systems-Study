## 1. Scope and Review Position

This document provides the initial deep literature synthesis for Applied Research Module 10.

It is designed as a **structured interdisciplinary evidence review**, not as a claim that every publication ever produced on lymphatic filariasis, Sri Lankan nutrition, immune function, socioeconomic health inequalities, cognition, or resilience has been exhaustively retrieved.

The literature was organised around the following domains:

1. lymphatic filariasis biology and morbidity;
2. Sri Lanka's elimination history and post-elimination morbidity;
3. lymphoedema, disability and quality of life;
4. nutrition–infection–immune interaction;
5. Sri Lankan dietary diversity and nutritional inequality;
6. socioeconomic determinants and physiological burden;
7. inflammation, allostatic processes and cognition;
8. resilience and adaptive-capacity measurement;
9. methodological gaps between these fields.

The purpose is not to manufacture a causal story from adjacent literatures. The purpose is to determine which links are well supported, which are plausible but indirect, and which remain genuinely open research questions.

---

# 2. Executive Synthesis

The literature supports a strong foundation for studying LF as a **multidimensional chronic health burden** rather than solely as an infection-transmission problem.

WHO distinguishes two broad programme objectives:

- interrupting transmission; and
- reducing suffering and disability among people already affected.

Sri Lanka's validation of elimination as a public-health problem in 2016 therefore does not mean that every historical consequence of LF disappeared. Contemporary Sri Lankan evidence continues to document morbidity among affected populations.

A second evidence base demonstrates that lymphatic filariasis can affect physical functioning, pain, emotional well-being, social functioning and quality of life. These outcomes create a plausible bridge from disease-specific pathology to broader human adaptive capacity.

A third literature shows that nutritional status and infection are bidirectionally related. Poor nutritional status can impair host defence and recovery, while infection can alter intake, absorption, requirements and nutrient losses. This literature is broad and biologically credible, but it is mostly **not specific to LF in Sri Lanka**. Therefore it supports a mechanistic background rather than a direct disease-specific conclusion.

A fourth body of evidence demonstrates substantial dietary and nutritional inequalities within Sri Lanka. Recent scoping evidence describes rice-dominant diets, limited variety of nutrient-dense foods, and marked variation by socioeconomic, demographic and geographic groups. Earlier studies similarly identify associations between dietary diversity, income, education, food access and nutritional status.

A fifth literature connects socioeconomic adversity with inflammation and, in some studies, cognitive outcomes. This evidence makes a multilevel systems model plausible, but it does not justify claiming that LF itself produces cognitive impairment or civilizational effects.

The resulting research gap is therefore specific:

> **There is a need to test whether disease-associated morbidity, nutritional adequacy, socioeconomic resources and physiological state can be integrated into a measurable population-level construct of Human Physiological Buffering Capacity, and whether this construct adds explanatory value beyond conventional disease, nutrition, disability and socioeconomic measures.**

# 3. Domain I — Lymphatic Filariasis as a Chronic Biological and Functional Burden

Lymphatic filariasis is caused by filarial parasites that damage lymphatic vessels and impair lymphatic function. Chronic manifestations include lymphoedema, elephantiasis and hydrocele.

WHO's current public-health framework explicitly recognises morbidity management and disability prevention as an essential component of LF elimination. Recommended care includes management of lymphoedema, treatment of acute inflammatory episodes, hydrocele surgery where indicated, and continuing care.

This is important for the present research because it establishes that LF burden is not exhausted by parasite transmission.

### Evidence interpretation

**Strongly established:**
- LF can damage lymphatic function.
- Chronic manifestations can produce disability and impaired quality of life.
- Morbidity management remains relevant after interruption of transmission.

**Not established by this literature:**
- that LF necessarily reduces an individual's physiological buffering capacity as defined in this project;
- that all patients experience systemic physiological depletion;
- that chronic LF directly produces cognitive impairment.

The distinction between these statements is central to scientific integrity.

---

# 4. Domain II — Sri Lanka: Elimination Does Not Equal Zero Morbidity

Sri Lanka implemented its National Programme to Eliminate Lymphatic Filariasis in 2002. Five annual rounds of mass drug administration with diethylcarbamazine and albendazole produced sustained reductions in infection, and WHO validated elimination of LF as a public-health problem in 2016.

Recent Sri Lankan research analysed filariasis-clinic passive case-detection data from 2006–2022 and hospital admission data from 2007–2022 to examine morbidity after elimination.

This evidence is especially relevant because it creates a natural research distinction:

```text
Transmission-level success
        ↓
Population-level elimination status
        ↓
Residual chronic morbidity
        ↓
Long-term disability and adaptation
```

The residual morbidity layer is precisely where a buffering-capacity framework may add analytical value.

### Critical methodological implication

A study performed only on current infection prevalence could miss a large component of the human burden relevant to this project.

A more appropriate design must capture:

- historical infection;
- current morbidity;
- duration;
- severity;
- recurrent acute attacks;
- disability;
- treatment access;
- functional consequences;
- socioeconomic consequences.

---

# 5. Domain III — Lymphoedema, Quality of Life and Psychosocial Burden

A Sri Lankan study of 141 patients with filarial lymphoedema in Colombo compared them with 128 healthy controls using SF-36 and GHQ-30 instruments.

Patients reported poorer physical functioning, greater role limitations due to physical health, poorer emotional well-being, poorer social functioning, more pain and poorer general health. Mental well-being was also worse than in healthy controls.

This is directly relevant because it demonstrates that LF-associated lymphoedema can extend beyond anatomical pathology into functional and psychosocial domains.

The appropriate inference is:

> LF morbidity can have multidomain human consequences.

The inappropriate inference would be:

> LF causes a generalised cognitive decline.

The second statement is not established by the cited study.

---

# 6. Domain IV — LF Immunology and Pathology

The immunology literature describes a complex interaction among filarial parasites, innate immunity and adaptive immunity.

Reviews describe antigen-specific Th2 responses, regulatory T-cell activity and IL-10-associated immune modulation in chronic infection. The development of pathology such as lymphoedema also involves inflammatory and vascular mechanisms.

A major review on filariasis and lymphoedema notes that only a minority of infected people develop clinically significant lymphoedema, and that susceptibility to pathology is heterogeneous.

This heterogeneity is critical for the buffering-capacity project.

It implies that:

```text
Same pathogen
        ≠
Same physiological trajectory
```

Potential modifiers include:

- host biology;
- duration and intensity of exposure;
- immune response;
- co-morbidities;
- socioeconomic conditions;
- nutrition;
- healthcare access;
- physical environment;
- recurrent secondary infection.

However, the literature does not permit these factors to be collapsed into a single causal model without empirical testing.

---

# 7. Domain V — Nutrition, Infection and Immune Competence

The nutrition–infection literature is extensive.

Classic and modern reviews consistently describe a bidirectional relationship:

```text
Poor nutritional status
        ↕
Altered host defence / immune competence
        ↕
Infection
        ↕
Altered intake, absorption, requirements and nutrient losses
```

This literature is strongest for broad relationships between undernutrition, micronutrient deficiency and infectious disease.

Protein-energy malnutrition and deficiencies involving nutrients such as vitamin A, iron and zinc can influence immune functions. At the same time, infection can increase nutrient requirements and alter nutrient utilisation.

More recent reviews emphasise that immune effects depend on nutrient, dose, baseline nutritional status, disease context and the presence of multiple deficiencies.

### Relevance to Module 10

The literature supports the use of **nutritional adequacy as a candidate buffering resource**.

It does not support:

- “immune boosting” as a scientific construct;
- a claim that a particular fruit cures LF;
- a claim that herbal plants replace treatment;
- a universal dietary recipe;
- a direct causal link between a specific food and LF morbidity.

The research should therefore use:

> **Nutritional Support of Host Physiological Resilience**

rather than “immune boosting”.

---

# 8. Domain VI — Sri Lankan Diet Quality and Nutritional Inequality

The most important new contextual evidence is the 2026 scoping review of diet, fruit and vegetable intake, and nutritional status in Sri Lanka.

That review identified 30 publications on diet, 13 on fruit and vegetable intake and 26 on nutritional status. It described rice-dominant diets and limited variety of nutrient-dense foods, with substantial differences across life stages and socioeconomic, demographic and geographic groups.

The review reported particularly important inequalities involving estate-sector residents, Indian Tamil populations and low-income groups.

This matters because the present module explicitly seeks to compare disease-associated buffering across resource strata.

### Important caution

Socioeconomic disadvantage is not equivalent to poor nutrition in every individual.

Likewise:

```text
Local availability
        ≠
Affordability
        ≠
Accessibility
        ≠
Consumption
        ≠
Nutritional adequacy
```

All five should be measured separately.

---

# 9. Domain VII — Dietary Diversity in Marginalised Sri Lankan Populations

A 2020 Sri Lankan study of 400 women of reproductive age in marginalised settings found poor dietary practices, macronutrient imbalance and low micronutrient intake. Approximately 83% of women in the urban sample did not meet minimum dietary diversity, and overall about 63% did not meet MDD-W and food-security criteria.

The study also reported relationships involving dietary diversity, income, education, food source, BMI and health status.

This provides strong contextual support for treating nutritional adequacy as a **resource-distribution problem**, not merely an individual choice.

The implication for LF research is that a chronic disease burden may interact with an already unequal nutritional environment.

The direction of that relationship must be measured.

---

# 10. Domain VIII — Food Access, Economic Stress and Nutritional Adaptation

Research from Sri Lanka also demonstrates that economic conditions can affect food security and dietary diversity.

A 2024 study examining low-income households during the economic crisis investigated cash transfers combined with mobile nutrition education.

Other Sri Lankan research has linked income, maternal education, household composition and employment to caloric adequacy.

These studies reinforce the need to include **economic access** in the buffering-capacity model.

A person cannot be expected to maintain physiological resilience through dietary choice alone if:

- food prices increase;
- income falls;
- transport becomes difficult;
- healthcare expenditure competes with food expenditure;
- household caregiving demands increase;
- chronic disease reduces employment capacity.

Thus, physiological buffering is partly embedded in an economic system.

---

# 11. Domain IX — Socioeconomic Position and Biological Embedding

Broader health literature shows that socioeconomic position is associated with differences in health through multiple levels:

```text
Economic resources
        ↓
Food / housing / healthcare / education
        ↓
Stress exposure and recovery opportunities
        ↓
Physiological processes
        ↓
Health outcomes
```

Reviews of socioeconomic health inequalities identify systemic inflammation, cellular processes and other biological pathways as candidate mechanisms.

Recent reviews also examine social determinants in relation to chronic inflammation and describe pathways involving cytokine regulation, HPA-axis alterations and other physiological mechanisms.

These findings support a **multilevel analytical strategy**.

They do not establish that any single socioeconomic variable causes a particular biological outcome.

---

# 12. Domain X — Inflammation, Social Adversity and Cognition

Recent evidence has begun to integrate social adversity, inflammation and cognitive outcomes.

A 2025 scoping review identified inflammation, allostatic load, genetic factors and biological ageing markers as possible mediating mechanisms connecting social adversity with cognition.

A 2023 review examined inflammation as a possible contributor to socioeconomic inequalities in neurocognitive disorders.

This literature provides a scientifically reasonable bridge between the physiological and cognitive layers of the broader programme.

However, the bridge remains probabilistic and heterogeneous.

The correct research question is:

> Under what conditions, through which mechanisms, and for which populations might chronic physiological burden become associated with measurable cognitive or behavioural differences?

The incorrect question is:

> Does LF automatically cause cognitive decline?

The literature does not support the latter.

---

# 13. Domain XI — Resilience and Adaptive Capacity

Resilience research increasingly treats resilience as a dynamic property rather than a fixed personal trait.

For Module 10, this suggests that buffering should be examined as a **state-dependent and resource-dependent capacity**.

Candidate components include:

- baseline physiological condition;
- acute perturbation;
- response magnitude;
- recovery time;
- repeated exposure;
- resource availability;
- social support;
- healthcare access;
- nutritional adequacy;
- sleep;
- physical activity;
- psychological resources.

A potentially useful conceptual distinction is:

```text
Resistance
= ability to limit deviation after a perturbation

Recovery
= ability to return toward baseline

Adaptation
= ability to reorganise after repeated or persistent perturbation

Buffering capacity
= proposed umbrella construct for the ability to absorb and regulate perturbation
```

These terms should not be treated as synonyms.

---

# 14. What the Literature Already Establishes

The literature provides relatively strong support for the following propositions:

1. LF can produce chronic lymphatic damage and disability.
2. Morbidity management remains important after transmission-level elimination.
3. LF-associated lymphoedema can reduce quality of life.
4. Nutritional status and infection interact bidirectionally.
5. Nutritional inadequacy can affect immune competence.
6. Sri Lanka contains meaningful nutritional and dietary inequalities.
7. Socioeconomic position influences food access and health.
8. Social adversity is associated with biological stress and inflammatory processes.
9. Inflammation and related biological processes may participate in some socioeconomic–cognitive relationships.

These are **literature-grounded background propositions**.

---

# 15. What the Literature Does Not Yet Establish for This Project

The following remain open:

1. whether LF-associated morbidity reduces a measurable Human Physiological Buffering Capacity;
2. whether a composite buffering construct adds explanatory value beyond existing measures;
3. whether nutritional adequacy mediates LF-related physiological effects;
4. whether socioeconomic position moderates disease-associated buffering;
5. whether chronic LF morbidity has measurable long-term cognitive effects independent of pain, sleep, depression, income and other confounders;
6. whether these pathways differ by urban/rural/estate setting;
7. whether elimination of transmission changes buffering trajectories over time;
8. whether population-level buffering can be linked to higher-order collective adaptive capacity.

These are precisely the areas in which the project should generate testable research.

# 16. Proposed Causal-Diagram Discipline

The literature strongly argues against a single linear causal chain.

A better starting point is a directed network:

```text
                    Socioeconomic Position
                       ↙     ↓      ↘
                  Food access  Healthcare  Education
                      ↓          ↓
                   Nutrition ← Disease burden
                      ↓          ↓
                Physiological state
                       ↓
                Buffering capacity
                    ↙       ↘
             Functional     Cognitive/
              capacity      behavioural state
                    ↘       ↙
                 Social participation
                         ↓
                  Household resources
                         ↺
```

The feedback arrow is intentional.

Disease can reduce income.

Reduced income can reduce food and healthcare access.

Reduced nutrition and recovery resources may increase vulnerability to additional stress.

This possibility requires longitudinal data.

---

# 17. Socioeconomic Stratification as a Core Variable

The module should not treat socioeconomic class as a descriptive afterthought.

At minimum, future studies should consider:

- household income or consumption;
- education;
- occupation;
- employment security;
- household size;
- caregiving burden;
- food expenditure;
- healthcare expenditure;
- transport;
- housing;
- water and sanitation;
- social protection;
- geographic access to MMDP services.

The central analytical question is:

> Does socioeconomic position alter the relationship between disease burden and physiological buffering?

This is a moderation question.

A second question is:

> Does disease burden alter socioeconomic position over time?

This is a reverse-causation / feedback question.

---

# 18. Why Population-Level Analysis Matters

An individual can appear clinically stable while living in a system that is losing adaptive capacity.

Population-level analysis allows investigation of:

- distribution;
- inequality;
- clustering;
- cumulative burden;
- household spillovers;
- geographic concentration;
- intergenerational effects;
- institutional demand.

The proposed construct therefore has two scales:

### Individual buffering

```text
Person → physiological perturbation → response → recovery
```

### Population buffering

```text
Population → distribution of resources and burdens
          → distribution of physiological states
          → distribution of recovery capacity
          → collective adaptive consequences
```

The second cannot be inferred simply by averaging the first.

---

# 19. Cognitive Layer: Conservative Interpretation

The broader programme proposes a physiological foundation beneath cognitive and social layers.

Module 10 should use this architecture cautiously.

Potential pathways include:

- pain → reduced attention;
- fatigue → reduced sustained cognitive effort;
- sleep disruption → altered attention and executive functioning;
- chronic stress → altered physiological regulation;
- reduced mobility → reduced activity and social participation;
- economic insecurity → cognitive load;
- recurrent illness → lost educational/work opportunities.

These pathways are plausible and supported to varying degrees by broader literature.

They should be tested rather than assumed.

---

# 20. Civilizational Layer: The Most Speculative Component

The proposed connection from individual physiological burden to civilizational adaptive capacity is the most distant inferential layer.

It should therefore be treated as a **long-horizon systems hypothesis**.

A defensible sequence is:

```text
Disease burden
→ individual function
→ household function
→ community participation
→ institutional demand
→ population adaptive distribution
→ possible civilizational implications
```

Skipping intermediate levels would constitute overreach.

No claim should be made that LF directly affects “civilization”.

# 22. Methodological Recommendations

A credible empirical programme should avoid constructing an arbitrary “buffering score” from unrelated variables.

Instead, researchers should compare several models:

### Model A — Conventional epidemiology
Disease severity → functional outcome.

### Model B — Resource-adjusted model
Disease severity + socioeconomic + nutritional variables → outcome.

### Model C — Buffering model
Baseline state + perturbation + recovery trajectory → outcome.

### Model D — Multilevel model
Individual + household + community + health-system variables.

### Model E — Longitudinal feedback model
Disease ↔ resources ↔ nutrition ↔ function over time.

If the buffering construct does not outperform conventional models, the construct should be revised or abandoned.

# 24. Confounding and Reverse Causality

Major confounders may include:

- age;
- sex;
- baseline disease status;
- comorbidities;
- obesity/underweight;
- diabetes;
- hypertension;
- anemia;
- smoking;
- alcohol;
- sleep;
- physical activity;
- mental health;
- occupation;
- education;
- household wealth;
- environmental exposure.

Reverse causality is especially important.

For example:

```text
Disease → income loss → poor diet
```

and

```text
Poor diet → poorer health → reduced disease recovery
```

may both occur simultaneously.

A cross-sectional association cannot distinguish these directions.

---

# 25. Research Ethics

Research involving affected communities should avoid portraying them as biologically deficient.

The analytical target is the **interaction between burden and resources**, not the supposed inferiority of a population.

Research should:

- involve affected communities;
- avoid stigma;
- use culturally appropriate measures;
- protect personal health data;
- avoid unnecessary biological testing;
- provide referral pathways where clinically relevant findings are identified;
- distinguish research from treatment;
- ensure that participation does not reduce access to routine care.

---

# 26. Overall Literature-Based Assessment

The literature supports the **background architecture** of Module 10 but does not validate the full Human Physiological Buffering Capacity construct.

The strongest scientific position is therefore:

> The module integrates several evidence-supported domains into a new testable research architecture, while keeping the cross-domain causal connections explicitly provisional.

The novelty claim should be limited to the **integration and operationalisation problem**, not to the discovery of LF, nutrition–infection interaction, socioeconomic inequality, inflammation, cognition, or resilience as separate fields.

---

# 27. Final Research Gap Statement

The central unresolved problem is:

> **How can the physiological burden of chronic disease, the distribution of nutritional and socioeconomic resources, the capacity for physiological recovery, and the downstream effects on human functioning be measured within one transparent multilevel framework without collapsing distinct scientific constructs into an unsupported composite?**

Applied Research Module 10 proposes Human Physiological Buffering Capacity as one candidate framework for answering this question.

Its validity remains an empirical question.
