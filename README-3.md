## 1. Purpose

This repository contains Applied Research Module 10 within the Human Physiological Buffering Capacity Research stream.

The module asks whether chronic or recurrent disease burden associated with lymphatic filariasis can be examined as one component of a broader **human physiological buffering problem at population level**, rather than only as an infectious-disease or clinical-treatment problem.

The module does **not** prescribe a diet, herbal remedy, nutritional recipe, or alternative treatment. It does not claim that food, plants, dietary diversity, or nutritional support can cure lymphatic filariasis.

Instead, it investigates a systems-level research proposition:

> Disease burden, nutritional adequacy, socioeconomic conditions, functional capacity, recovery resources, and access to appropriate medical and morbidity-management services may interact over time to shape the physiological and adaptive capacity of individuals and populations.

The research then asks whether such population-level physiological conditions could plausibly propagate into higher-order cognitive, social, institutional, and civilizational layers.

This is a research question, not an established causal pathway.

## 3. Why Lymphatic Filariasis in Sri Lanka?

Sri Lanka provides an unusually informative setting for this research because the country achieved WHO validation of elimination of lymphatic filariasis as a public-health problem in 2016, while chronic morbidity among previously affected people remains relevant.

A Sri Lankan study using filariasis-clinic passive case-detection data from 2006–2022 and hospital admission data from 2007–2022 specifically examined continuing morbidity after elimination as a public-health problem. This distinction is fundamental to the module:

```text
Elimination of LF as a public-health problem
        ≠
Disappearance of all historical disease-associated morbidity
```

WHO likewise emphasizes that morbidity management and disability prevention must continue for people living with chronic manifestations such as lymphoedema and hydrocele.

## 4. Core Research Proposition

The central proposition is:

> **Population-level Human Physiological Buffering Capacity may be shaped not only by acute physiological state, but also by chronic disease burden, nutritional adequacy, socioeconomic resources, functional impairment, recovery opportunities, and access to sustained health and social support.**

For LF, the proposed research chain is:

```text
Past / present LF-related burden
        ↓
Lymphatic dysfunction + recurrent inflammatory/infectious episodes
        ↓
Functional limitation / pain / fatigue / disability
        ↓
Reduced work capacity, income, mobility, social participation
        ↓
Reduced access to food, healthcare, education, recovery resources
        ↓
Potential nutritional and physiological vulnerability
        ↓
Reduced buffering capacity under additional stressors
        ↓
Potential effects on cognitive / behavioural / social functioning
        ↓
Population-level adaptive consequences
```

This diagram is deliberately presented as a **testable systems hypothesis**. Individual links may be weak, nonlinear, bidirectional, confounded, or absent.

## 5. Nutritional Boundary

Nutrition is included as a **physiological-support layer**, not as a treatment substitute.

The relevant literature supports a broad relationship between nutritional status, immune competence, infection, inflammation, and recovery. However, the existence of this general relationship does not establish that a particular food or plant prevents, treats, or cures LF.

Accordingly:

- “nutritional adequacy” is preferred to “immune boosting”;
- “nutritional support of host physiological resilience” is preferred to therapeutic claims;
- foods are distinguished from medicinal plants and pharmaceuticals;
- locally available food is not assumed to be universally affordable;
- affordability, accessibility, seasonality, cultural acceptability, safety, and preparation burden must be measured;
- dietary recommendations are outside the scope of this conceptual module unless a future clinical/nutrition research team develops and ethically validates a specific intervention.

## 6. Population Stratification

The module explicitly proposes comparison across socioeconomic and resource strata without assuming that any group is biologically homogeneous.

Candidate strata include:

1. economically disadvantaged households;
2. lower-middle and middle-income households;
3. relatively affluent households;
4. urban populations;
5. rural populations;
6. plantation/estate-sector communities;
7. populations with different levels of healthcare access;
8. people with different degrees and durations of LF-associated morbidity.

The objective is to study **distribution of buffering resources and burdens**, not to label a population as inherently resilient or vulnerable.

## 7. Time-Scale Architecture

### Short term
- acute inflammatory episodes;
- pain and fatigue;
- temporary functional limitation;
- dietary disruption;
- reduced work capacity;
- acute healthcare expenditure.

### Medium term
- recurrent morbidity;
- persistent lymphoedema;
- reduced mobility;
- chronic fatigue or functional limitations;
- loss of income;
- reduced dietary diversity;
- reduced access to preventive and rehabilitative resources.

### Long term
- disability;
- household economic consequences;
- educational and occupational effects;
- intergenerational consequences;
- social participation;
- chronic health burden;
- possible cumulative effects on cognitive and adaptive capacity.

These are candidate pathways for longitudinal investigation, not claims that all patients experience them.

## 8. Cognitive and Civilizational Boundary

The module asks whether physiological burden can become relevant to higher-order human systems.

The intended architecture is:

```text
Disease / physiological burden
        ↓
Individual physiological adaptive capacity
        ↓
Attention / fatigue / cognitive-resource availability
        ↓
Behavioural and interpersonal functioning
        ↓
Collective participation
        ↓
Institutional adaptive capacity
        ↓
Civilizational adaptive capacity
```

Existing literature supports relationships among socioeconomic adversity, inflammation, allostatic processes, health, and cognition. However, this evidence should not be used to claim that LF directly causes cognitive decline or civilizational effects.

The proposed higher-order links therefore remain **research hypotheses** requiring longitudinal and multilevel evidence.

## 9. Key Research Questions

- **RQ1:** How should disease-associated Human Physiological Buffering Capacity be operationalised at population level?
- **RQ2:** Which LF-related morbidity indicators are most informative for physiological buffering?
- **RQ3:** How do nutritional adequacy and dietary diversity interact with disease burden and socioeconomic resources?
- **RQ4:** Are socioeconomic inequalities associated with differentiated buffering trajectories among people affected by LF?
- **RQ5:** Do recurrent morbidity, disability, or functional limitation predict measurable changes in recovery capacity?
- **RQ6:** Which effects are acute, which are cumulative, and which are reversible?
- **RQ7:** Do cognitive and behavioural outcomes mediate or moderate the relationship between physiological burden and social participation?
- **RQ8:** Which household- and community-level factors buffer adverse effects?
- **RQ9:** Does elimination of transmission produce a delayed reduction in morbidity burden, or do chronic cases require a separate long-term pathway of support?
- **RQ10:** Can the construct of Human Physiological Buffering Capacity add explanatory value beyond conventional measures such as BMI, hemoglobin, income, disability, or disease severity?

## 10. Main Hypotheses

### H1 — Disease-load hypothesis
Greater LF-associated chronic morbidity is associated with lower measured physiological buffering capacity, after appropriate adjustment for age, sex, comorbidity, socioeconomic status, and other confounders.

### H2 — Resource-buffering hypothesis
Greater access to adequate nutrition, healthcare, rehabilitation, income stability, safe housing, sanitation, and social support is associated with greater physiological adaptive capacity.

### H3 — Inequality hypothesis
Disease-associated physiological burden is distributed unequally across socioeconomic and geographic populations.

### H4 — Time-scale hypothesis
Acute disease episodes, chronic morbidity, and long-term disability produce distinguishable temporal signatures.

### H5 — Cognitive-interface hypothesis
Physiological burden may be associated with cognitive or behavioural outcomes through fatigue, pain, sleep disruption, stress, inflammation, reduced activity, or other pathways.

### H6 — Feedback hypothesis
Disease burden and socioeconomic deprivation may form bidirectional feedback loops rather than a simple one-way causal chain.

None of these hypotheses is presented as established fact.

Future empirical work may combine:

- LF history;
- antigen/parasite status where clinically indicated;
- lymphoedema grade;
- hydrocele status;
- acute dermatolymphangioadenitis episodes;
- duration of morbidity;
- treatment and MMDP access.

### Physiological
- anthropometry;
- blood pressure;
- resting heart rate;
- selected laboratory markers;
- nutritional biomarkers;
- sleep/recovery indicators;
- physical activity;
- functional capacity;

### Nutritional
- dietary diversity;
- household food security;
- food expenditure;
- nutrient adequacy;
- meal frequency;
- access to fruits, vegetables, legumes, animal-source foods and other nutrient-dense foods;
- seasonality.

### Socioeconomic
- income;
- education;
- employment;
- occupation;
- household size;
- transport access;
- healthcare expenditure;
- social protection;
- geographic accessibility.

### Cognitive / psychosocial
- validated cognitive measures;
- fatigue;
- pain;
- psychological distress;
- social participation;
- work functioning;
- quality of life.

No single variable should be treated as an adequate surrogate for the full construct.

## 12. Research Design Direction

A strong empirical programme would preferably use:

1. longitudinal cohort designs;
2. repeated physiological measurements;
3. validated LF morbidity assessments;
4. socioeconomic stratification;
5. nutritional assessment;
6. multilevel modelling;
7. mediation and moderation analysis;
8. sensitivity analyses for reverse causation;
9. missing-data analysis;
10. preregistration and transparent protocols;
11. external replication;
12. where feasible, linkage to routine health-system data.

Cross-sectional data can identify associations but are insufficient by themselves for establishing temporal causal chains.

## 13. Literature Position

The literature review identifies five relatively mature evidence domains:

1. **LF biology and morbidity management** — well established.
2. **Persistence of morbidity after elimination of transmission** — established and directly relevant to Sri Lanka.
3. **Nutrition–infection–immune interaction** — substantial general evidence.
4. **Socioeconomic inequalities in nutrition and health** — substantial evidence, including Sri Lanka.
5. **Relationships among social adversity, inflammation, physiological load and cognition** — growing evidence, but more heterogeneous.

The major gap is not the claim that any one of these fields is new.

The research gap is the **integration and operational testing of these domains into a disease-associated population-level Human Physiological Buffering Capacity framework**, with explicit time scales and socioeconomic stratification.

---

## 14. Falsification and Negative Results

The project should be considered scientifically productive even if the central construct is not supported.

Potential falsification outcomes include:

- LF morbidity shows no independent association with buffering measures;
- nutritional indicators do not explain additional variance;
- socioeconomic stratification disappears after adequate adjustment;
- cognitive associations are explained entirely by pain, sleep, depression, income, or other confounders;
- no longitudinal accumulation is observed;
- the proposed construct adds no explanatory value beyond existing health measures.

A null result should be treated as evidence against the relevant hypothesis, not as a failure of the research programme.

---

## 15. Medical and Ethical Boundary

This repository is not a clinical guideline.

It must not be used to:

- replace antifilarial treatment;
- replace WHO-recommended morbidity-management services;
- recommend unvalidated herbal treatments;
- claim that particular foods cure LF;
- delay medical evaluation;
- infer individual prognosis from the conceptual framework;
- stigmatise affected populations.

Any future human-subject research requires appropriate ethics review, informed consent, data protection, clinical oversight where necessary, and community participation.
